# Bit Decoder

Turn the previous lesson into code: encode and decode two's complement patterns, simulate
overflow, split a 32-bit word into the sign, exponent and fraction of an IEEE 754 float, and
encode and decode UTF-8, all with shifts and masks instead of library calls. When you finish,
`0x3DCCCCCD` will no longer look like noise: it is the float closest to 0.1. Press **Enter** to
create the kata folder and open it.
